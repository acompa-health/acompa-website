# Acompa Website Positioning Design

**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Status:** Approved design; ready for implementation planning after user review

## Objective

Revise Acompa's introductory website so that a first-time visitor quickly understands two things:

1. Acompa is a credible undertaking led by an unusually consequential group of people.
2. Acompa is attempting a fundamental redesign of primary care, not another incremental digital-health product.

The site must communicate this without calling the founders prestigious, describing the vision as bold, or leaning on conventional global-health or AI-startup signals.

## Audience and intended impression

The audience includes funders and institutional partners, ministries and implementation partners, and exceptional potential recruits or collaborators. These visitors share the same first question: is Acompa real and credible?

The intended sequence of impressions is:

1. Something unusually serious is being assembled here.
2. The team is attempting a much larger change than another digital-health product.
3. The team has already built, operated, studied, and delivered consequential parts of the system it now wants to redesign.

The primary failure mode to avoid is looking like a conventional global-health NGO. Secondary failure modes are looking like an overhyped AI startup or like an elite team admiring its own resumes.

## Source material

The design is grounded in:

- The current one-page Acompa website and its existing brand assets.
- The final mission, vision, and values tab in the Acompa Mission Values and Vision document.
- The team's PIH bios document.
- The Adaptive Care Network solution architecture document.
- Publicly verifiable biographies and institutional sources for the four founders.

The mission and vision below are authoritative website copy unless the team changes the source document.

**Mission**

> We are reimagining healthcare systems to be AI-native to care for the most vulnerable communities.

**Vision**

> A world where poverty and geography no longer decide the quality of anyone's care.

## Chosen direction

The governing direction is **quiet authority, radical premise**.

It combines three ideas:

- Editorial restraint and exact language establish institutional seriousness.
- The founders' histories provide evidence that the premise is credible.
- A simple care-system model makes the ambition concrete without exposing an internal solution architecture.

Two alternatives were considered and rejected as primary directions:

1. **Four perspectives, one conviction.** This would make the founders' convergence the central story. It communicates the rarity of the team, but risks becoming founder-centric and self-congratulatory.
2. **The system, redrawn.** This would organize the entire page around a care-system blueprint. It is visually and intellectually compelling, but risks implying product and architecture decisions that remain under design.

The selected direction borrows a concise team-convergence section from the first alternative and one public-facing system illustration from the second.

## Core proposition

The website should imply this idea without using it as a slogan:

> The people reimagining this system have already built, operated, studied, and delivered consequential pieces of it.

The deeper care-model premise is:

> Primary care should be responsible for everyone, not merely available to whoever reaches a clinic.

In public language, avoid the specialist term *denominator* unless it is immediately explained as everyone the health system is responsible for.

## Narrative architecture

The page builds one argument:

**Conviction -> credibility -> changed premise -> enabling system -> governing values -> destination**

### 1. Header

- Retain the Acompa mark and name.
- Use a small number of section links aligned to the new structure, such as Purpose, Approach, and Team.
- Do not add a prominent startup-style call to action.

### 2. Hero: the conviction

- Acompa Health is the signature, not the dominant headline.
- The mission is the dominant statement and should be used nearly or entirely verbatim.
- Supporting copy, if any, is limited to one short line that clarifies the care-model ambition. It must not introduce product categories.
- Remove the current voice-first product description from the hero. Voice remains central, but it is not the company's entire identity.

### 3. Vision: the destination

- Present the exact vision immediately after the hero.
- Use minimal explanation so mission and vision read as a matched pair.
- Do not substitute the current clinic-door headline for the authoritative vision.

### 4. Why this is credible

- Introduce the founders early, before a detailed explanation of products or methods.
- Begin with one collective paragraph spanning frontline clinical delivery, rural hospitals, national health programs, globally deployed digital platforms, applied AI, company-building, and operational scale.
- Present four concise evidence-led profiles. Each profile gets one consequential proof point and a restrained expanded bio or outbound profile link.
- Use names of institutions, systems, and companies only when they prove something: examples include PIH, CDC, Antara, CommCare/Dimagi, Captricity/SS&C, WHO, Open Chat Studio, and the relevant universities.
- Use verbs such as *built*, *led*, *deployed*, *treated*, *founded*, and *scaled*. Avoid adjectives such as *world-class*, *renowned*, *exceptional*, or *prestigious*.
- Avoid a logo wall and a degree parade.

### 5. A different premise for primary care

Replace the current product-card framing with a concise contrast.

**Primary care today**

- Care begins when someone arrives.
- Each visit is treated as an episode.
- The patient carries information between services.
- Missed care often disappears from view.

**Primary care, redesigned**

- Care begins with everyone the system is responsible for.
- The system reaches out, remembers, and follows through.
- Information moves instead of patients whenever possible.
- Every interaction strengthens the next.

This section communicates the scale of the ambition without describing it as radical or invoking admired figures as a comparison.

### 6. How the enabling system works

The homepage should not expose the internal component names Natural Language Services, Care Context Exchange, Care Workflow Orchestration, Care Access Line, CHW Companion, Facility Workspace, or County Console.

Compress the architecture into three public promises:

1. **Reach people in the language and channel available to them.**
2. **Bring the right context and intelligence to every care decision.**
3. **Keep responsibility visible until care is complete.**

Voice is a pervasive mode of interaction across all three promises:

- Patients can reach care from any phone and in the language they use.
- CHWs can prepare for and document household work with less typing.
- Clinicians can reconstruct less history and document more naturally.
- Work can continue across screens, voice, devices, and changing connectivity.

The platform is an enabling and coordination layer, not a replacement record system. It should be described as helping organizations safely use the best available AI while strengthening systems already in use. It connects context, intelligence, and work across people and systems while keeping accountable humans responsible for clinical decisions.

Do not imply that Acompa must build every model, own every interface, replace CommCare or OpenMRS, or determine every deployment architecture.

### 7. What governs the work

Present the eight authoritative values as four editorial movements rather than eight equal cards. Preserve the source language as closely as possible.

**Care in daily life**

- Health is improved in daily life.
- Care is designed with patients at the center.

**Care that learns**

- Every interaction strengthens the next.
- Humans anchor learning loops.

**Care through relationship**

- Great healthcare requires accompaniment.
- Relationships are the foundation of care and technology amplifies relationships.

**Complexity and responsibility**

- The system carries complexity before the people do.
- Humans are accountable for clinical decisions.

The group labels are website organization, not replacements for the authoritative value statements.

### 8. Close

- Return to the authoritative vision rather than inventing another variation of the mission.
- Add one restrained invitation to talk, collaborate, or work with Acompa.
- Remove the current redundant footer tagline unless it serves a clear navigational purpose.

## Current content treatment

### Remove or replace

- Replace the hero headline treatment in which “Acompa Health” carries the page.
- Replace “We build technology that helps primary care reach people where they are” with the authoritative mission.
- Remove “Voice-first, human-centered systems...” from the hero.
- Replace “Excellent care should not depend on getting through the clinic door” with the authoritative vision.
- Remove “Infrastructure for care...” and avoid *infrastructure* as homepage framing.
- Remove the three equal product cards.
- Remove the four equal operating-principle cards.
- Remove generic functional founder labels such as Clinical implementation, Technology and systems, Digital health and AI, and Operations and partnerships.
- Replace the current short biographies with evidence-led profiles.
- Replace the closing headline and paragraph with the authoritative vision and a restrained invitation.

### Preserve or recast

- Preserve accompaniment as a governing belief, using the authoritative value language.
- Preserve trust and relationships under the relationship values.
- Recast design for constraints as the system carrying complexity before people do.
- Preserve human clinical accountability using the authoritative values.
- Preserve voice as a pervasive access and work capability rather than a standalone product category.
- Preserve the idea that technology serves the care model, but express it through the new narrative rather than a separate slogan.
- Keep open standards and interoperability in technical or partnership material unless later made part of the authoritative public values.

## Visual direction

The visual character is **quietly formidable**: humane without NGO earnestness, technically ambitious without AI-startup theatrics, and prestigious without behaving like a resume.

### Layout

- Favor an editorial reading experience over a collection of cards.
- Use strong typographic hierarchy, generous space, and a deliberate scroll sequence.
- Reduce borders, rounded boxes, gradients, and category-colored panels.
- Maintain a simple, dependency-light, responsive one-page site.

### Typography

- Pair a distinctive editorial display face with a precise sans-serif for explanation and evidence, subject to an implementation review of licensing, performance, and legibility.
- Use typography to distinguish thesis, evidence, and explanation.
- Avoid the current effect in which all-Inter typography makes every section feel equally functional.

### Color

- Retain the existing deep green, warm paper, blue, and restrained lime as the starting palette.
- Use fewer pale card colors and less gradient.
- Use color to create rhythm and orientation, not to decorate categories.

### Visual motif

- Consider one original continuous-line or thread motif connecting person, household, CHW, clinician, facility, and health system.
- The motif should communicate accompaniment, continuity, shared context, and responsibility for everyone.
- It must remain a supporting device, not become an abstract software architecture diagram.

### Motion

- Motion is optional and must reveal continuity or handoff rather than decorate the page.
- Do not use floating particles, glowing brains, animated dashboards, spinning globes, or similar AI and venture-tech shorthand.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` if motion is implemented.

## Image decision

The current cat's-cradle hero image remains the default for the first implementation plan. Image replacement is a separate approval gate and must not block the positioning work.

A replacement should be proposed only if it clearly outperforms the current image by:

- showing agency rather than vulnerability;
- suggesting relationship, coordination, language, or care in motion;
- feeling specific to Acompa rather than to global health generally;
- avoiding beneficiary-as-symbol and conventional NGO imagery;
- accommodating the mission legibly across desktop and mobile crops; and
- using a source with clear rights and appropriate consent.

Do not conduct a broad open-source image search merely to produce alternatives. A replacement should come from a compelling concept, commissioned or owned photography, or a deliberately created original visual.

## Content and tone rules

- Prefer exact, declarative sentences.
- Let consequences and specificity establish credibility.
- Do not call the ambition radical, revolutionary, audacious, world-class, or prestigious.
- Avoid “empowering communities,” “impact at scale,” “leveraging synergies,” and adjacent global-development boilerplate.
- Avoid generic AI claims and language suggesting autonomous clinical decision-making.
- Do not describe vulnerable people as passive beneficiaries.
- Explain the transformed premise of care in ordinary language before introducing any systems language.
- Keep the homepage at introductory depth; detailed product architecture belongs in partner materials.

## Accessibility and responsive behavior

- Preserve the semantic landmark and heading structure of the current site.
- Maintain keyboard-visible focus states and descriptive accessible names for outbound profile links.
- Verify text contrast across image overlays and the retained palette.
- Ensure the mission and vision reflow cleanly without orphaned lines at common mobile widths.
- Do not encode the care-model contrast or the system promises solely through color or motion.
- If the continuous-line motif is decorative, hide it from assistive technology. If it conveys unique meaning, provide an equivalent textual explanation.
- Test at representative mobile, tablet, laptop, and large desktop widths.

## Scope boundaries

This design covers the content, information architecture, and visual direction of the existing single-page introductory website.

It does not include:

- publication of the internal adaptive-care architecture;
- selection of vendors, models, or deployment architecture;
- a product documentation site;
- detailed partnership case studies;
- a news or content-management system;
- a recruiting portal;
- a broad image-licensing search; or
- production implementation before the design spec and implementation plan are separately approved.

## Acceptance criteria for the later implementation

1. The authoritative mission and vision appear exactly or with explicitly approved deviations.
2. All eight values appear in their approved website organization without changing their meaning.
3. The team appears early and is described through verifiable evidence rather than prestige adjectives.
4. The page communicates the shift from episodic, reactive care to responsibility for everyone the health system serves.
5. Voice appears as a cross-system mode of access and work, not as Acompa's sole product.
6. The enabling platform is understandable without exposing internal component names or implying replacement of existing record systems.
7. The visual result avoids NGO and AI-startup cliches.
8. The current hero image is retained unless a separately approved concept meets the replacement criteria.
9. The final site remains responsive, keyboard accessible, readable with reduced motion, and dependency-light.
10. No unrelated repository content or brand assets are changed.
